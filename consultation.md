Implement a complete, fully functional **Video Consultation and Physical Consultation Booking System** for LegalEase — Legal Help. Made Easier. — within the existing project. First, inspect the current codebase, database schema, authentication, citizen dashboard, lawyer dashboard, admin dashboard, appointment workflow, payment integration, notification system, and existing UI components. Reuse the existing architecture and database entities wherever possible. Do not create a separate application, duplicate existing tables, or break existing features. Use the project's existing stack, preferably Next.js App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, Supabase/PostgreSQL, and server-side services. The feature must work end-to-end with real database records, secure video rooms, payment verification, server-controlled consultation timers, commission accounting, appointment completion, and mandatory citizen ratings. Do not deliver only a UI mockup or buttons with simulated behavior.

**1. FREE AND OPEN-SOURCE VIDEO CONSULTATION TECHNOLOGY:** Use **Jitsi Meet** as the preferred video consultation solution because it is open-source and supports browser-based video meetings without requiring citizens or lawyers to install a dedicated application. First evaluate the existing project and choose the simplest reliable integration. Prefer a self-hosted Jitsi Meet deployment for greater privacy, control, and predictable long-session availability if server resources and deployment support are available. Alternatively, use the public Jitsi Meet service for development or a prototype, clearly documenting its availability, privacy, and operational limitations. Do not assume that a public Jitsi room provides guaranteed privacy, access control, uptime, or contractual data protection. Use a suitable secure Jitsi integration, such as the official IFrame API, where appropriate. If authenticated meeting access, participant identity enforcement, or server-controlled room restrictions are required, configure the relevant Jitsi deployment capabilities rather than assuming that an unguessable room URL alone guarantees security. Generate a unique, unpredictable room identifier for each eligible video appointment, associate it with the appointment record, and restrict access through the application's server-side authorization checks and the configured meeting-security mechanism. Never expose server secrets, Jitsi signing keys, or private credentials in client-side code. Do not record consultations unless a separate, explicitly authorized recording feature is implemented. If Jitsi configuration is unavailable, display a clear setup state rather than falsely showing a working meeting.

**2. CONSULTATION TYPES:** Provide two booking options: **Online Video Consultation** and **Physical Consultation**. The citizen must explicitly select the desired consultation type when booking. Video consultations are paid according to the lawyer's published fee and the applicable platform commission policy. Physical consultations must be free to book through LegalEase, with no online consultation fee or platform commission charged for the physical booking. Clearly display the consultation type, fee, duration, cancellation rules, date, time, and booking conditions before confirmation. A physical consultation must not accidentally create a payment request, video room, online meeting timer, or video-consultation commission. Physical appointments should still follow the normal lawyer-availability, acceptance, cancellation, and completion workflow.

**3. CUSTOMIZABLE VIDEO CONSULTATION DURATION:** Allow the citizen to select a video consultation duration between **60 and 180 minutes**, inclusive. The selectable duration must be configurable in the booking interface, using a simple dropdown, segmented control, or equivalent accessible input. Support durations such as 60, 75, 90, 105, 120, 135, 150, 165, and 180 minutes, or another clearly defined set of selectable increments that remains within the permitted range. The selected duration must be saved in the appointment record and used consistently for booking availability, fee calculation where applicable, the video-session timer, and consultation completion. Set a sensible default, such as 60 minutes, but let the citizen choose another permitted duration. Validate the minimum and maximum duration on the server; never rely only on frontend validation. Reject durations below 60 minutes, above 180 minutes, or values that do not follow the configured selection rules. Ensure the selected time slot is genuinely available for the entire requested duration, including any configured buffer between appointments. Prevent overlapping bookings and double booking using server-side availability checks and appropriate database constraints or transaction-safe logic. If a lawyer cannot accommodate the selected duration, ask the citizen to choose another duration or time slot.

**4. LAWYER ACCEPTANCE AND PAYMENT WORKFLOW:** Implement the following strict sequence for paid video consultations: the citizen selects an eligible, administrator-verified lawyer, chooses video consultation, selects an available date, time, and duration, reviews the fee breakdown, and submits an appointment request. The appointment remains in a pending state until the lawyer accepts it. When the lawyer accepts the request, the appointment transitions to an accepted state and the citizen receives a notification to complete payment. The appointment must not become eligible for joining until the payment provider or trusted server-side payment workflow confirms that the payment has succeeded. After confirmed payment, update the appointment and payment records, issue the appropriate confirmation to the citizen and lawyer, and enable the video consultation join option for the authorized participants. If the lawyer rejects the request, the citizen cancels within the applicable policy, or the payment fails or expires, update the appointment and payment state according to the defined workflow. Do not treat an unverified client-side callback or screenshot as proof of payment. Prevent duplicate charges, duplicate payment records, and repeated processing of payment events. Handle payment-provider webhooks securely, verify their authenticity, and use idempotency controls wherever supported. If payment integration is not yet configured, implement the necessary provider abstraction and database workflow, clearly identify the credentials and setup required, and never pretend that real money has been received.

**5. PAYMENT COLLECTION AND ADMIN ACCOUNT:** For video consultations, collect the full payable amount through a trusted payment provider and configure the payment flow so that funds are collected for the platform or routed according to the provider's supported marketplace or connected-account functionality. The intended business rule is that the citizen pays first, the consultation takes place, and the lawyer receives the net payable amount after the consultation is completed and the applicable commission is deducted. However, distinguish the intended accounting workflow from actual movement of money: recording a payment in the database does not itself transfer money to an administrator's bank account. Before implementing automatic transfers, inspect the payment provider and its country-specific availability, settlement rules, supported payout methods, and marketplace capabilities. If the provider supports the required transfer workflow, implement it securely using the provider's official server-side APIs. If it does not, keep the collected payment in the provider's supported settlement flow and provide an accurate commission and payout ledger without claiming that an unsupported transfer has occurred. Never store card details or banking credentials unnecessarily, and never expose payment secrets to the browser.

**6. CONFIGURABLE PLATFORM COMMISSION:** Build an administrator-controlled commission configuration, using the existing admin dashboard where possible. Allow the authorized administrator to configure the platform commission as a percentage and, if needed, a fixed amount, with validated limits and an effective date. Apply the configured commission to eligible paid video consultations only. Physical consultations must remain free and commission-free. Before the citizen pays, display a transparent breakdown of the applicable video-consultation fee and any additional charges actually configured and legally applicable. Define clearly whether the lawyer's published fee is the total amount charged to the citizen or the lawyer's gross consultation fee before commission. Use one consistent policy across booking, payment, reporting, and payout calculations. Store a snapshot of the applicable fee and commission configuration on the appointment or associated financial record at payment time, so future configuration changes do not silently alter an existing transaction. Calculate all amounts using safe decimal or integer minor-unit arithmetic, not floating-point calculations. For example, if the configured consultation fee is PKR 10,000 and the commission is 10%, the commission is PKR 1,000 and the lawyer's net share is PKR 9,000, assuming the fee is defined as the gross amount before commission and there are no other applicable charges. Show the actual configured values in the interface rather than hardcoding this example.

**7. SERVER-CONTROLLED CONSULTATION TIMER:** Build a consultation timer that uses the confirmed appointment start time and the booked duration, with server-authoritative timestamps. The citizen and lawyer interfaces should display the scheduled duration, elapsed time, remaining time, and appointment status. Start the session timing according to a clearly defined policy, such as the scheduled start time once the paid appointment becomes eligible and the authorized consultation session opens. Handle late joining, early joining, one participant being absent, page refreshes, reconnections, and browser closure without resetting or extending the timer improperly. Do not calculate the official elapsed time solely using a browser-side JavaScript timer, because the user could refresh or manipulate it. Persist the relevant session timestamps and derive the displayed timer from trusted server time and saved session state. If the application needs to distinguish scheduled duration from actual participation time, store both separately. Where the Jitsi deployment supports trustworthy participant-presence events, use them to record join and leave events; do not claim to measure exact speaking time or continuous participation if that capability is not available. Clearly distinguish the booked duration, the elapsed appointment time, and the observed participant presence. Provide sensible grace-period and extension rules, if approved by the business policy, and prevent unauthorized users from extending a session.

**8. CONSULTATION ROOM ACCESS:** Show a **Join Video Consultation** button on the citizen dashboard and lawyer dashboard only when the appointment is accepted, payment is confirmed, the scheduled access window is open, and the requesting user is an authorized participant. Apply any configured early-join window and late-join grace period consistently. Before the appointment becomes eligible, show a countdown or appointment details rather than an active join button. On joining, open the protected Jitsi room through the configured integration and display the appointment details, duration, remaining time, connection status, and relevant instructions. Support leaving and rejoining without creating a new appointment or resetting the official timer. Prevent citizens from joining other citizens' rooms, lawyers from joining appointments assigned to other lawyers, and unauthorized users from accessing meeting metadata or links. Enforce all checks on the server, not only by hiding the button. If the meeting service fails, show a meaningful error and follow a defined rescheduling or support procedure instead of falsely marking the consultation as completed.

**9. AUTOMATIC TIME LIMIT AND SESSION END:** The consultation should be governed by the booked duration, between 60 and 180 minutes. When the allocated time expires, show clear warnings as the end time approaches, for example at 10 minutes and 5 minutes remaining, followed by an end-of-session notification. At the official end time, prevent the appointment from continuing as a normal in-scope session and display an appropriate completion or time-expired state. Use server-side session validation to enforce time limits. If the selected Jitsi deployment supports reliable room-control mechanisms, integrate those controls to end or restrict the meeting at the configured time. Do not assume that a frontend countdown alone can terminate a Jitsi meeting for every participant. If room-level termination requires self-hosted Jitsi or an additional service, document and configure that requirement. Define what happens when a consultation needs additional time: the parties must follow an explicit extension or new-booking workflow, with any additional payment and availability checks completed before the session is extended. Never extend paid sessions automatically or charge extra without the citizen's explicit authorization.

**10. LAWYER COMPLETION AND ADMIN VERIFICATION:** After the consultation ends, provide the lawyer with a **Mark Consultation as Completed** action on the lawyer dashboard. The lawyer must be able to mark the appointment completed only when the appointment is in an eligible state and the relevant session rules are satisfied. Validate completion on the server using appointment status, confirmed payment, scheduled duration, session timestamps, and the available participation evidence. Record who completed the appointment and when. Do not automatically assume that a lawyer's click proves that the entire booked duration occurred. If the lawyer marks completion before the booked duration has elapsed, apply a defined early-completion policy and record the actual timestamps and reason; prevent false reporting that the full duration was delivered. If there is insufficient evidence to determine whether the consultation took place, mark the appointment for review or require the appropriate confirmation rather than fabricating proof. Provide a configurable policy for exceptional situations, such as both parties agreeing to finish early, a technical failure, or a participant failing to attend. Make completion information visible to the authorized administrator, including booked duration, session start and end times, observed join/leave events where supported, payment status, and the lawyer's completion timestamp. Preserve an audit trail of important changes. The admin must be able to review disputed or suspicious records and take authorized actions without silently rewriting the historical session data.

**11. COMMISSION SETTLEMENT AND AUTOMATIC TRANSFER:** After a paid video consultation is validly marked completed and any required validation or dispute checks pass, calculate the platform commission and lawyer net amount using the saved transaction snapshot. If the payment provider supports delayed transfers, split payments, or connected-account payouts for the relevant account and country, implement the supported settlement flow through secure server-side provider APIs. Use idempotent payout requests, unique transaction references, webhook verification, retry handling, and reconciliation so that a repeated event cannot transfer the same funds twice. Track financial states such as payment received, settlement pending, eligible for payout, transfer initiated, transfer successful, transfer failed, refunded, or disputed, using the existing canonical schema where available. The administrator must be able to see the gross fee, commission, net lawyer share, settlement state, provider transaction reference, and relevant timestamps. If automatic transfer is not supported by the configured payment provider, mark the amount as eligible for manual or supported settlement and clearly show that no transfer has occurred. Do not claim that funds were transferred to the administrator's bank account merely because the appointment was completed. Configure the provider's actual fee collection and settlement arrangement so the platform commission reaches the intended platform account through a legally and technically supported payment flow. **Do not charge the citizen again at consultation completion.** The citizen's original successful payment must be linked to the appointment and reused for commission accounting and settlement.

**12. PHYSICAL CONSULTATION WORKFLOW:** Physical consultation booking must be available without an online booking charge or LegalEase platform commission. The citizen selects physical consultation, chooses an eligible lawyer, and books an available date and time. The lawyer accepts or rejects the request, and both parties receive the relevant notifications. Once accepted, show the appointment details and the physical meeting location if the lawyer has authorized its disclosure. Do not require payment before confirmation, create a video room, or display video timers for physical consultations. Allow the lawyer to mark the physical appointment completed through the normal completion workflow, preserving the relevant timestamp and audit history. If the platform later supports offline payments directly between lawyer and citizen, keep those transactions separate from the platform's online payment system unless an authorized, explicit payment workflow is added. Ensure the free physical booking rule is enforced in the backend, not merely reflected in the interface.

**13. MANDATORY CITIZEN RATING:** After an appointment has been validly marked completed, require the citizen to submit a rating from **1 to 5 stars** for the lawyer before treating the appointment's feedback process as complete. The written feedback must remain optional. Provide a simple star-rating interface and an optional text field. The citizen must not be able to rate a lawyer for an uncompleted, cancelled, rejected, or unrelated appointment. Enforce eligibility server-side, allow only the citizen associated with the appointment to submit the rating, and prevent duplicate ratings for the same appointment unless a documented policy explicitly allows an edit. If the platform requires a rating before booking another appointment or accessing a particular completion-dependent feature, enforce that restriction narrowly and explain it clearly. Do not block access to unrelated essential account functions. Provide a rating reminder and a clear pending-rating state in the citizen dashboard. Save the rating and optional feedback to the database and make the resulting review visible in the lawyer dashboard and admin review tools according to moderation rules. Recalculate average ratings from genuine eligible reviews only. Do not fabricate ratings or permit the lawyer to edit the citizen's feedback.
